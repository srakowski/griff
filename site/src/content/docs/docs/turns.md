## The Roguelike template

The **Roguelike** template is a menu showing the deepest floor reached, with Play and Quit, and a `Crypt` scene made anew every game by a **Dungeon Generator** (Seed 0) on a tilemap of stone walls and flagstone floors: the hero starts in the first room and the stairs wait in the furthest, with three or four slimes, a bat or two, a potion or two and some gold scattered through the other rooms. It's turn-based: the hero steps a cell a turn (Tab waits one), and every monster steps or hits once after. The hero walks into monsters to fight them (a **Bump Attack**, two hits for a slime, one for a bat); slimes (`Prefabs/Slime.prefab`) notice the hero within five cells if they can see them, bats (`Prefabs/Bat.prefab`) anywhere within eight, flying diagonally, and either hits the hero by walking into them. Gold counts up, potions go in the hero's inventory and 1 drinks one (four hit points back). The stairs make the next floor, keeping the hero's health and potions. The HUD shows the hero's health bar, the floor (a counter with **Count Floor**, keeping the deepest), gold and potions; it's dark but for the hero's lantern. A **You Died** panel opens on the hero's Death Message `Game Over` with the floor reached and the deepest, Again and Menu, and the pause menu is there too, with no scripts.

## Turns

**Entity → Create Turn Order** makes a game turn-based: a **Turn Order** whose text in the top-right corner says whose turn it is, and an **End Turn** button in the bottom-right corner. Its **Sides** take turns in order (`Player, Enemy`, or `Blue, Red` for two people at one keyboard), and whatever acts gets a **Turn Taker** naming its **Side**. Off its side's turn, a turn taker waits: the player's keys don't reach it (nor its scripts' `Input`), and its Patrol, Chase, Grid Chase, Grid Patrol, Attack and Shooter hold still. It still falls, gets pushed and gets hurt, and everything with no turn taker (moving platforms, particles, a ball still rolling) runs in real time as ever, so a turn can be a stretch of real-time play, as in Worms. A person's turn ends when they press End Turn (Tab or the gamepad's Back: its **End Action**) or click a button whose **Action** is End Turn (faded while they can't), or when its **Turn Time** runs out (0 waits for them). The **Computer Sides** (`Enemy`) can't be ended that way: their turn lasts **Computer Turn Time** seconds, long enough for patrols to walk and turrets to fire. Give a turn taker **Actions** to limit what it does in a turn: each attack swing and each shot uses one (scripts use them with `Act()`), it waits once it has used them, and once everyone on a side has, the turn passes by itself. **Between** turns nobody acts for a moment, so a last shot lands. A side with nobody left is skipped, and once a side that had turn takers loses the last of them it's beaten: its **Defeat Message** goes out once (`{0}Defeated`: `RedDefeated`, with the side as its data), for a Victory or Defeat panel to open on. Each turn sends its **Message** (`{0}Turn`: `PlayerTurn`, then `EnemyTurn`, with the side as its data, so a panel or a sound player can greet it) and plays its **Sound**; its **Format** fills its text (`{0}'s turn`, with `{1}` the **Round** and `{2}` the seconds left). **Current**, **Round**, **Time Left** and each turn taker's **Actions Used** are saved with the scene, so rewinding goes back to whose turn it was. In a network game the host runs the turns, and with the Multiplayer's **Teams** each player's character is on its player's team (sides `Blue, Red` for teams `Blue, Red`); only the players on that side can end its turn.

## Roguelike turns

On a grid, a turn taker with **Actions** takes turns a step at a time: each step of its **Grid Movement** uses an action (bumping a wall doesn't), and a **Grid Chase** or **Grid Patrol** steps once an action, passing when it has nowhere to go (out of range, waiting at a point, blocked) and nothing to swing at, so its side's turn ends straight away rather than after Computer Turn Time. With one action each and **Between** at 0, everything moves when the player moves: the player steps, every enemy steps (or swings) once, and it's the player's turn again, the steps gliding at the same time. A swing lands before its side's turn passes, so a monster's blow can't be dodged by stepping away; the next turn also waits for a mover still gliding from its last step, so play goes at the pace of the slowest mover. Holding a way walks a step a turn, a way pressed while the computer takes its turn is taken as the turn comes back, and Tab (End Turn) waits a turn. A grid patrol waits whole turns at its points (its **Wait**, rounded up). **Create Turn Order** in a scene with grid movers sets all this up: each grid mover gets a turn taker with one action (players on the first side a person plays, chasers and patrols on the first the computer plays), Between is 0, the text counts turns (`Turn {1}`) and the button says **Wait**; grid players and enemies made after it take turns too. Online, a player's step on their own machine is their action on the host.

## Tactics

**Entity → Create Tactics Unit** makes a unit for a tactics game: a box in its side's color with a **Grid Movement**, a **Health** of 3, a **Tactics Unit** and a **Turn Taker** with one action, on whichever side has the fewest units so far (so making four gives two even teams, Blue down the left and Red down the right). In a scene with no turn order it also makes one (`Blue, Red`, with the computer playing Red, and an End Turn button), and a **Tactics Controls** to pick units with. On your side's turn, click one of your units: the cells it can walk to light up blue (up to its **Move**, `4`, around walls and other units, which block friend and foe alike), and the cells it could hit from them red (things from **Min Range** to **Range** steps away, `1` for a sword; an archer is Range `3`, Min Range `2`; ranges reach over walls). Hover a cell to see the way it would go, and click it to walk there. Then click a foe in reach to attack it (its **Damage**, with an **Attack Sound**, a **Hit Effect** and a **Hit Stop**), or the unit itself to wait; with nothing in reach it's done as soon as it gets there. Clicking a foe straight away walks up to the nearest cell it can hit it from and attacks. A right click (or Cancel, Shift or the gamepad's B) takes the walk back, or lets the unit go. Attacking or waiting uses the unit's action, it's dimmed (**Done Tint**) until its side's next turn, and once every unit on the side has had its go the turn passes. Hovering any unit shows faintly how far it reaches, health bars sit under the units, and the arrow keys or the stick move a cursor that Space (or the gamepad's A) clicks with (**Confirm Action** and **Cancel Action**), all colored to taste. A unit's foes are the units on other sides, or with **Layers** set, whatever has health on them (a gate to break down). Scripts read a unit's **Reachable**, **Targets**, **Can Act** and **Is Done**, give it orders with `MoveTo((x, y))`, `Attack(entity)`, `Wait()` and `UndoMove()`, hear **Moved**, **Attacked** and **Done**, and pick a unit with the controls' **Selected**. Whether a unit has moved is saved, so rewinds keep its go; online the host moves the units and each player picks on their own screen, their clicks sent to the host.

## The computer's side in tactics

On a side the turn order's **Computer Sides** names (Red, from Create Tactics Unit; clear it for two people at one keyboard), the computer takes each unit's go in turn, the unit nearest a foe first: its reach lights up for the controls' **Computer Pause** (`0.5` seconds) so you can see what's coming, then it attacks the best foe it can reach with a walk and a hit (one it can finish off first, then the weakest, then the nearest), walking up to it, or with nothing in reach walks as far as it can toward the nearest foe, around walls. Each unit's **Stance** says how it plays: **Charge** (always comes at you), **Guard** (stays put until you come within a walk and a hit of it) or **Hold** (never walks, only attacking what's already in range: a tower, an archer on a wall). Set the turn order's **Computer Turn Time** to `0` (as Create Tactics Unit does) so its turn lasts as long as its units need. The host plays the computer's side in a network game.

## The Tactics template

The **Tactics** template is a menu (Play and Quit) and a `Battle` on a field of grass with trees and rocks in the way, painted on a tilemap: your two knights (Move 4, 6 hit points, 2 damage up close) and two archers (Move 3, shooting two or three cells off) start on the left, and the computer's three orcs (charging), a goblin archer guarding the far side and another holding the rocks start on the right. It's played as the Tactics section above says, Blue first; the turn text counts the rounds (`{0}'s turn · Round {1}`), health bars show who's hurt, and hits throw sparks with swings and shots to hear. Beat every orc and goblin and **Victory** opens (on `RedDefeated`, the turn order's Defeat Message); lose all four of yours and it's **Defeat** (`BlueDefeated`). Both offer Again and Menu, and the pause menu is there too, with no scripts.

## Dungeons

**Entity → Create Dungeon** makes a dungeon: a tilemap with a **Dungeon Generator**, 48 cells across (**Width**) and 32 down (**Height**), centered in the view and showing a dungeon made from a new seed, its rooms and corridors cut from solid wall so they show without a tileset. As the game starts it fills its tilemap with a new floor: up to **Rooms** (`9`) rooms, each **Min Room Size** to **Max Room Size** cells across and down, a wall apart, joined by corridors **Corridor Width** wide (each room to its nearest, so they're short), plus **Loops** more between rooms already joined, so there's more than one way round. Floors are **Floor Tile** (-1 leaves them empty; list a floor tile in the tilemap's **Background Tiles** so nothing collides with it) and the cells round them **Wall Tile**, a row deep, or everything else with **Fill Walls**. The entities named in **Players** (`Player`) are put in the middle of the first room, the **Exit** (a staircase, say) in the room the longest walk from it, and **Spawns** are scattered through the other rooms, one per line: a prefab and how many (`Enemy 4`, `Potion 1-2` picks a number between, `Gold` is one), never on the exit or each other. A player stepping onto the exit's cell (once a grid mover's step ends) goes down to the next floor (untick **Descend On Exit** to go only when told): a new dungeon, the last floor's spawns cleared away, the players in the new first room. So does sending its **Next Floor Message** (say `Next Floor`, from a button or a script). **Floor** counts the floors, and each one after the first plays **Floor Sound** and sends **Floor Message** (`Floor`, with 1). A **Counter** with **Count Floor** shows the floor (`Floor {0}`), even in a panel that was closed as the floors went by, and with **Save Best As** keeps the deepest. The same **Seed** always makes the same dungeon, each floor its own; 0 (to begin with) makes a new one every game. **Entity → Generate Dungeon** makes the selected dungeon in the editor to see it, putting the players and exit in place as one undo step (with Seed 0, a new one each time; the game still makes its own). Untick **Generate On Play** to play the map as painted, with the floors after it made. Spawns go inside a `Spawns` entity in the dungeon's. Scripts call `NextFloor()` and `Generate()` (this floor again), read `RoomList`, `RoomAt(x, y)`, `ExitCell` and `UsedSeed`, and hear `Generated`. The tiles and Floor are saved with the scene, so a rewind goes back to the floor it was on. In a network game the host makes the floors and everyone sees them.
