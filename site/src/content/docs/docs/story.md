## Doors

A **Door** on a trigger takes whoever touches it (on its **Layers**, `Player`) to its **Scene** (the level's exit) or, with no scene, to the entity named in its **Target** (a teleporter, a door into a house), fading out and in over **Fade** seconds. Tick **Needs Interact** and it waits for the Interact action (E, Enter or the gamepad's Y). Having arrived through one, you have to step off before a door takes you anywhere again, so a pair of portals never sends you back and forth. A door to another scene says where you arrive there with its **Arrive At** (the door back, so two rooms join both ways; see [Doors between scenes](/docs/mashups/#doors-between-scenes)). **Prompt** names an entity (a "Press E" text, its child) that shows only while someone can use it. **Needs Flag** keeps it shut until a flag is set (`MetKing`, or `not MetKing` for while it isn't): see [Flags outside dialogue](#flags-outside-dialogue).

## Dialogue with characters

**Entity → Story → Dialogue Box** makes a panel at the bottom of the screen whose text types itself out, with a portrait and a name tag above it for whoever is talking (see [Speakers and portraits](/docs/story/#speakers-and-portraits)). Give a sign or a character a trigger collider and a **Dialogue**: its **Lines** (one per row) are said in the box when the player presses Interact beside it, or straight away with **Start** set to **Touch**. Interact finishes the line being typed, then shows the next (so does a click or a tap on the game, so a talk can be played with the mouse or on a touch screen); after the last the box hides itself and it sends its **Message** (`TalkedToKing`). **Pause Game** stops everything while it's said (or, with **Pauses** naming an entity, only it and everything inside it: see [Pausing part of the game](/docs/ui/#pausing-part-of-the-game)), **Once** says it only the first time, and **Prompt** works like a door's. With **Start** set to **Scene Start** it starts by itself as the scene starts and needs no collider at all (an intro, a visual novel's script); once said it isn't said again until the scene is loaded again. **Pause Game** stops everything while it's said (or, with **Pauses** naming an entity, only it and everything inside it: see [Pausing part of the game](/docs/ui/#pausing-part-of-the-game)), **Once** says it only the first time, and **Prompt** works like a door's. The box is hidden while the game runs until someone talks, and a **Dialogue Box** with a **Wrap Width** wraps long lines onto more rows. Where a talk has got to (its **Is Talking** and **Line**, under State) is saved with the scene, so rewinding in the editor goes back into the middle of it, without doing the lines' tags again; tick Is Talking and pick a Line to start the game partway through a talk. **Entity → Edit Dialogue...** edits the lines in the Dialogue Editor, a line at a time (see [The Dialogue Editor](/docs/visual-novels/#the-dialogue-editor)).

## Dialogue choices

A row of **Lines** starting with `>` is a choice, offered under the line before it; several in a row are picked between. The player moves between them with the arrow keys, W and S, the D-pad or the stick (the picked one has an arrow by it), or points at one, and picks with Interact, a click or its number key. A choice ending in `-> Name` goes to the row `# Name`, which starts a part of the talk that's only reached that way: the lines before a part end where it starts. A choice with nowhere to go goes on after the choices, a row `-> Name` goes there by itself, and `-> End` ends the talk. So a shopkeeper is:

```
Want a sword?
> Yes -> Buy
> No -> Bye
# Buy
That'll be ten gold.
-> End
# Bye
Safe travels!
```

Parts can go back to earlier ones, to ask again (the Top-down template's guide does). Going to a part that isn't there stops the game as soon as the talk starts, naming it. Start a row with `\` to say it as it is, `>` or `#` and all.

## Dialogue flags

Flags remember what was said and picked. A row can start with tags in square brackets: `[set MetKing]` sets a flag and `[clear HasKey]` clears one when the line is said (or the choice picked), `[if MetKing]` says the line only when the flag is set and `[if not MetKing]` only when it isn't, and `[send OpenGate]` sends a message, for a gate that opens on one answer and not another. A choice's tags go after its `>` (`> [if HasKey] Open the vault -> Vault`), and it's only offered while they hold; a go-to with an `[if]` is only followed while it holds, and a row of tags alone just does them. Choices go under the last line said of those just above them, so two greetings can share them (and when none of those lines is said, neither are the choices). So a king who remembers you is:

```
[if MetKing] Back again, are we?
[if not MetKing] [set MetKing] Welcome, stranger.
> [if HasKey] I found the key! -> Key
> Farewell -> End
# Key
[clear HasKey] [send OpenVault] Splendid! The vault is yours.
```

Flags are kept in the game's save data under their own names, so they're still set the next time the game runs (**Game → Clear Save Data** forgets them while you test). Capitals don't matter. Scripts read and change them with `Game.HasFlag("MetKing")` and `Game.SetFlag("HasKey")` (or `SetFlag("HasKey", false)`), and a toggle's **Save As** is a flag too. A saved game keeps the flags set with dialogue, triggers or `SetFlag` when it was saved, and loading it puts them back as they were (see [Saving and loading](/docs/visual-novels/#saving-and-loading)); Skip, Auto and a log of the lines said work for any dialogue (see [Skip and Auto](/docs/visual-novels/#skip-and-auto)). Anything else in brackets (`[sighs] Fine.`) is said as it is.

## Speakers and portraits

Give someone a **Speaker** and their name (its **Name**, or the entity's) and **Portrait Image** show over what their **Dialogue** says, in the box's **Name Tag** and **Portrait** (named by the box's **Dialogue Box** component; each hides when there's nothing to show, like a sign's lines). A line starting with a speaker's name, `You: I'll help!`, is said by them instead, so the player answers back (give the player a Speaker called `You`), and a Speaker on an empty entity, even a hidden one, is a narrator or a voice on the radio. A line whose start matches no speaker (`Note: ...`) is said as it is. `Ava (happy): Hi!` is said by Ava with that expression: on a visual novel's stage her face changes (see [Visual novels](/docs/visual-novels/)), and scripts read it as the dialogue's `Expression`.

## Switches

**Entity → Level → Switch** makes a lever. Name what it opens in its **Targets** (commas between names): while it's on, a **Mover** moves (a gate set to **Once** slides open, and closes again when it's switched off), a **Spawner** or **Shooter** runs, particles emit, an animation plays and a **Door** unlocks; anything else simply appears. **Hides** names what disappears while it's on: a barrier, a wall of spikes. It all starts switched off, so a bridge a lever brings out isn't there until it's pulled. Its **Mode** is **Lever** (Interact flips it, with a **Prompt** like a door's), **Plate** (on while something on its **Layers** stands on it: `Player, Crate` for crates too) or **Button** (on for good once touched; shoot it by setting **Layers** to `Bullet`). **On Time** turns it off again after that many seconds, for a timed button. It turns its sprite its **On Tint** (and its **On Frame**, for a lever drawn both ways), plays its **Sound** and sends its **On Message** and **Off Message**. Set its **Beats** and it flips itself on the music's beat as well (see [Keeping the beat](/docs/audio/#keeping-the-beat)). **Needs Flag** stops it being worked until a flag is set (its prompt stays hidden; a trigger's **On** still turns it on). If several switches name the same thing, it's on only while all of them are, so a gate can need two plates held down. Names are looked for inside the switch first, so a prefab can switch its own parts; the Scene view draws a selected switch's links. What a switch (or a wire into **Power**) turns on and off is whatever its target's components mark `[Powered]`, so a script component's own `Running` can be switched too.

## Triggers

Anything can set off anything with no code. Components name what they do as **actions**: a **Switch**'s **On**, **Off** and **Toggle**, a **Door**'s **Open** and **Lock**, a **Spawner**'s **Spawn**, a **Shooter**'s **Fire**, a **Mover**'s **Step** (to its next point, waiting there for the next), a **Health**'s **Damage**, **Heal** and **Kill**, a **Counter**'s **Add** and **Reset**, a **Turn Order**'s **End Turn**, a **Panel**'s **Open** and **Close**, a match or falling board's **Pause**, **Resume** and **Restart** (and a match board's **Shuffle**), a **Move History**'s **Undo** and **Restart**, a dungeon's **Next Floor**, a **Dialogue**'s **Start**, a **Sound Player**'s **Play**, and a trigger's own **Set Flag**, **Clear Flag**, **Load Scene** (to the scene named in its **With**, fading out and in: `Ending` once the last talk is over), **Play Minigame** (the scene named in its **With**, on top of this one), **Win Minigame** and **Lose Minigame** (in a minigame, ending it with a score) and **Forget Scene** (see [Minigames](/docs/mashups/#minigames) and [Scenes that remember](/docs/mashups/#scenes-that-remember)), a **Minigame**'s **Play** and **Stop**. Give any entity a **Trigger** (Gameplay) for each thing it reacts to: when its **Message** arrives (from anyone, or only **From** a named entity or something inside it), it does the action in **Do** on its **Targets** (on itself without any), **With** a value for an action that takes one (`2` for Damage, `MetGuide` for Set Flag, `Ending` for Load Scene), or the message's own number with **Use Message Number** (a beat's count, the points just scored). **Do** is a list of what its targets can do, flagged in yellow when none of them can; the Inspector shows a line on what it does ("When Score arrives from Board A, Damage 2 on Boss."), and the Scene view draws an arrow from it to what it drives, labelled the same way, and one from what it hears. So "a match fires the cannon" is a trigger on the cannon waiting for the board's **Matched**, doing **Fire**; "the beat ends the turn" is one waiting for the clock's **Beat**, doing **End Turn** on the turn order. A Contraptions **Wire** into an action (its **Input** set to `Spawn`, `Fire`, `Damage`) does it once each time its signal comes on, with the signal as its amount, as well as holding **Power** or **Show** while it's on. Actions are done where the game is run (offline, or on the host; a player's own panels and sounds on their machine), and triggers setting each other off stop after a few steps.

## Messages to part of the scene

Messages go to everyone, unless what sends them says otherwise. Give an entity a **Message Scope** and the messages it and everything inside it send (a board's Score, a switch's On Message, a goal's Covered) go only to its **Target** (itself when that's empty) and everything inside it (**To** is **Children** to begin with), or to the target alone (**Entity**): with two match boards side by side, each with a scope and its own score counter inside it, each counter counts only its own board. Counters, triggers, sound players, panels and scripts' `On` outside the scope don't hear them.

## Outputs

Anything can read anything with no code. Components name what they know as **outputs**: a match board's **Score**, **Swaps**, **Moves Left** and **Chain**, a falling board's **Score**, **Lines**, **Level** and **Game Over**, a goal's **Covered** and the scene's **Goals Covered** out of its **Goals**, a move history's **Moves**, a dungeon's **Floor**, a turn order's **Round**, **Turn** (whose it is), **Time Left** and **Left:Enemy** (how many of a side's turn takers are left), a beat clock's **Beat**, **Bar** and **Beat In Bar**, a faction's **Amount:Gold** (any resource after the colon), **Units** and **Buildings**, a hotbar's **Selected Item**, **Selected Count** and **Blocks:Dirt**, an inventory's **Count:Key**, a counter's **Count**, **Reached**, **Left**, **Best** and **New Best**, health's **Health**, a body's **Speed** and **Height**, a switch's or wire button's **On**, a gate's **Out** and a sensor's **Out** and **Count**. Some say what they're out of: a board's score out of its **Target Score**, health out of its **Max**.

Three things show them: a **Text** (**Track** and **Output**; its **Value** says how it reads, `{0}` the output and `{1}` what it's out of, with a format after a colon, `{0:000}` or `{0:0.0} m/s`, and a value without them shows just the output; a true-or-false output shows On or Off, or with a format is 1 or 0, so `{0:'New best!';;''}` says something only while it's on; it's the one that shows outputs that give a name, such as whose turn it is), a **Counter** (counting it, and doing something when it reaches a value: see [Pickups and scores](/docs/ui/#pickups-and-scores)) and a **Bar** (filling to it). A Contraptions **Wire** carries any output that gives a number or true or false (1 or 0) into an input. Without a **Track**, each reads the entity it's on or the nearest it's inside with that output, so a text inside the player shows its speed. **Output** is picked from a list of what the entity it reads knows, flagged in yellow when it knows nothing called that; `MatchBoard.Score` names one component's when two have the same. The Scene view draws an arrow from what's read to what reads it, labelled with the output. A script component's own properties join in with `[Output]` (see [Scripting](/docs/scripting/)). Reading an output every step makes no garbage, and in a network game each machine reads its own copy of what the host sends.

## Flags outside dialogue

Flags aren't only dialogue's. A trigger's **Set Flag** and **Clear Flag** set and clear them when a message arrives (with the flag's name as its **With**), and a **Switch**, **Door**, **Spawner** and **Panel** can each need one: with **Needs Flag** `PowerOn` a lever does nothing, a door stays shut, a spawner waits and a panel won't open until it's set; `not PowerOn` (or `!PowerOn`) only while it isn't. So "the gate opens once you've talked to the king" is a door needing the flag the king's dialogue sets, and "the boss comes once the counter reaches ten" is a counter's **Reach Message** heard by a trigger that sets the flag a spawner needs. Flags are kept in save data, so they last between runs (see [Dialogue flags](#dialogue-flags)).

## Keys and inventory

Give a **Pickup** an **Item** (`Key`) and whoever collects it carries it in an **Inventory** (one is added to them if they have none, unless they're on a team whose items they carry: see [Items and resources](/docs/mashups/#items-and-resources)). A **Price** (`5 Coin`) makes it a shop's ware, collected only by someone who can pay. A **Door** with a **Key** stays shut to anyone without one, and the first trip through uses it up; a switch with a **Key** works the same, so a locked block is a solid box with a **Button** switch that needs a key and **Hides** itself. A **Counter** with an **Item** shows how many the player holds (`Keys: {0}`). Inventories are saved with the scene, so rewinding gives back the keys picked up since, and you can start a playtest already holding a key by typing `Key` into the player's **Items**. With **Save As** (`Items`) an inventory is kept in the save data too, so what the player carries is still there the next time the game runs, and an inventory saved as the same name in the next scene starts with it (a player going from room to room; **Game → Clear Save Data** forgets it while you test), as a health's and a counter's **Save As** carry theirs (see [Carrying inventory, health and counts](/docs/mashups/#carrying-inventory-health-and-counts)). Items can be used, too: the inventory's **Item Uses** say what each does, one per line (`Potion: heal 3`, `Bomb: spawn Explosion`, `Scroll: send Teleport`; several separated by commas): `heal` gives back hit points, `spawn` puts a prefab where the holder stands, and `send` sends a message with the holder as its data. With **Number Keys** (on to begin with) the number keys use them, 1 the first item it holds that has a use, 2 the second, and so on, each use taking one out and playing its **Use Sound**; a potion that only heals is kept while its holder's health is full. Taking turns, using one is an action and only happens on the holder's turn. Scripts call `Use("Potion")`, read `Usable` and `EffectsOf(item)`, and hear `Used`.

## Drag and drop

Give something a **Draggable** and the player picks it up with the mouse: press on it (its collider, or its sprite if it has none), carry it, and let go. It's drawn over everything while it's held, and keeps the spot it was grabbed by. Give a slot, a hole or a place on the table a **Drop Target**: something dropped on it (by its middle) lands in its middle, sliding there over its **Slide Time**, and the target lights up in its **Hover Tint** while something it would take is held over it. A target **Accepts** the **Kind**s named (`Red Gem, Blue Gem`; empty takes anything) up to its **Capacity** at a time (0 for any number: a bin, a discard pile); anything else dropped on it slides back where it came from. Untick **Snap** for an area things stay where they're let go in, and tick **Swap** on inventory slots so dropping on a full one swaps the two. Off a target, a draggable stays where it's let go, lands in the middle of a square of its **Grid** size (1 lines it up with a tilemap's cells), or with **Only On Targets** goes back. **Stays On Target** fixes a jigsaw piece once it's in place. Draggables play their **Pick Up Sound** and **Drop Sound** and send their **Pick Up Message** and **Drop Message**; targets send a **Drop Message** when something lands and a **Take Message** when it's taken off, and every target with the same **All Full Message** sends it once they're all full: every piece in place, the puzzle solved. What's on a target is whatever rests with its middle inside it, so things placed on a target in the Scene view start on it. In a network game the host carries things for each player by their own mouse, and two can't hold the same thing.

## Drag and drop with buttons

For a game played with a controller or keys, give a cursor (or a character) a **Grabber**. Press its **Action** (`Interact`: E, Enter or the gamepad's Y) and it picks up the draggable under its hand, or else the nearest one whose middle is within its **Reach**; press again and it puts it down where it's held, onto a drop target as the mouse would. What a press would pick up lights up in its **Highlight**. A put-down the mouse would send back (on a full target, one for other kinds, or off targets for something that only goes on them) does nothing instead, so it keeps holding the thing and plays its **Refuse Sound**; put down on a full target that swaps, it picks up what was there, so a controller can shuffle an inventory around. Tick **Hold To Carry** to carry only while the button is held. Its **Hold Offset** is where it holds things: its X is in front of it (the side it faces, turning as it does) and its Y up, so `0, 1` carries a crate over a character's head and `0.8, 0` in front of it; a cursor keeps `0, 0`. Move a cursor with a **Top-down Movement** (or a **Grid Movement**, to step from slot to slot), which ignores gravity, so a cursor works in a game that has some. Grabbers send a **Pick Up Message** and **Drop Message** with what they moved, and scripts read `Holding` and `InReach`, hear `PickedUp` and `Dropped`, and call `Press()`. In a network game each player's grabber answers to their own controller.

Every template has a pause menu. Both the Platformer and Top-down players carry a sword (X). The Platformer template has a lift up to a ledge where a slime patrols (two swings defeat it) and a lever brings out a bridge with more coins, a spring up to two ledges that crumble under you, a checkpoint flag, a sign to read, and a player with three hearts who comes back at the flag. The Top-down template is a dim dungeon lit by flickering torches and the player's lantern; in it a bat chases you once it sees you, portals take you across the room, there's someone to talk to, a key across the room opens a vault, and a crate pushed onto a plate brings out a bonus gem. The Isometric template is a sunny ruined courtyard seen at a slant, on an isometric map: a floor that doesn't collide and a layer of stone walls, a hedge and low walls along the front that's solid and sorts each diagonal among the sprites, so the hero walks behind the old shed's walls and in front of them. Its people stand on the diamonds (their colliders are flat boxes at their feet), gems float over their cells (one in the shed, one behind the hedge, three in the corners), a slime chases you on sight and finds its way round the walls, a crate pushed up into the hedge's corner presses a plate that brings out a bonus gem, and the gardener talks to you from the diamonds round them. All three templates' players move with no script and dash with Shift, leaving streaks; the Arcade template's bullets leave streaks too, and its scripts are the place to see how a game is coded.
