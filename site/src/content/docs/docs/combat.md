## Health and hazards

Add a **Health** to anything that can be hurt and set its **Max**. After a hit it flashes white and blinks, and can't be hurt again for its **Invincible Time**; at zero it dies, sends its **Death Message** (say `PlayerDied`) and is destroyed (untick **Destroy On Death** to play a death animation first). Add a **DamageOnTouch** to whatever hurts: spikes and lava (it keeps hurting while you stand in it), enemies, bullets. Its **Layers** says who it hurts (`Player` on spikes, `Enemy` on the player's bullets; empty hurts anything with health), **Knockback** pushes the target away, and **Destroy On Hit** spends a bullet. A collider on a child entity counts for the nearest Health above it, so a sword or a hurtbox can be its own child, and it never hurts its owner. The Arcade template's ship has three hearts, and its enemies and bullets use both.

## Moving platforms

Add a **Mover** to a platform (or a saw, a lift, a gate) and give it **Points** to visit, as offsets from where it's placed: `4 0` goes four units right and back, `0 6` is a lift, `4 0; 4 3; 0 3` goes round a square. It moves at **Speed**, waits **Wait** seconds at each point and eases in and out of them (untick **Smooth** for a steady saw). **Mode** is **PingPong** (there and back the same way), **Loop** (straight back to the start and round again) or **Once** (a door sliding open). Whatever stands on it rides along. Its **Time** is how far into its trip it is: give a row of platforms different times so they don't move in step. Set its **Beats** and it steps from point to point on the music's beat instead (see [Keeping the beat](/docs/audio/#keeping-the-beat)). The Scene view draws each mover's path; select it to drag its points, add more and take them off. Make a lift a one-way platform so players can walk through it at the bottom.

## Enemies

A **Patrol** walks its body back and forth at **Speed**, turning at walls and, with **Turn At Edges**, before walking off a ledge. Give it a **Left** and a **Right** to keep it to a stretch of a long floor: it turns that far either side of where it's placed (0 walks on until a wall or an edge). The Scene view draws its route, and you can drag its ends. Its **Walked** is how far along from where it was placed it is: the game keeps it up to date (knock it back and it walks back to its route), and setting it starts it part of the way along. A **Chase** goes after its **Target** (`Player`) once it's within **Range**, and gives up once it's **Give Up** further away than that. With **Needs Sight** it has to see the target first, past any walls; with gravity it runs left and right after it; without, it heads straight there, or finds its way around a tilemap's walls if **Around** names it (`Level`). **Stop Distance** keeps it back, for an enemy that shoots. Give an entity both and it patrols until it spots you. Both flip the sprite to face where they're going (draw it facing right). Add a **DamageOnTouch** and they hurt.

## Grid enemies

**Entity → Characters → Grid Enemy** makes an enemy for a grid game: a **Grid Movement** a little slower than the player's (its **Step Time**), a **Grid Chase**, and an enemy **Attack** whose `Hitbox` child hurts the `Player` layer. A **Grid Chase** steps after its **Target** (`Player`) once it's within **Range**, tile by tile and the shortest way round walls, solid things and other grid movers (it waits behind one blocking a corridor), and gives up once the target is **Give Up** further away than that; with **Needs Sight** it has to see the target first. It stops **Stop Distance** steps away (1 is next to it, 2 keeps a cell between them) and turns to face it, so an attack swings its way. At 0 it walks into its target's cell, and if the target is solid it bumps into it each step instead: a roguelike's bump attack (its **Bumped** event and **Bump Sound**). A **Grid Patrol** walks a grid mover round its **Points**, in cells from where it's placed (`4 0`, or `4 0; 4 -3; 0 -3` round a square), finding its way round walls between them, waiting for whatever stands in its way and pausing **Wait** seconds at each. Its **Mode** goes there and back, round in a loop or once, like a Mover's; switches start and stop it (**Running**), **Leg** starts it part of the way round, and the Scene view draws its route; select it to drag its points from cell to cell, add more and take them off. Give an entity both and it patrols until it spots you, then finds its way back to its route once it gives up. Either one steps on its own, never with the player's keys; in a network game the host moves it, after the nearest player. For a roguelike's fighting, give grid movers a **Bump Attack**: stepping into something with **Health** on its **Layers** (`Enemy`) hurts it by **Damage** instead of bumping, with a lunge toward it and back over a step's time, its **Sound**, an **Effect** prefab on what it hits and an optional **Hit Stop**; holding the way keeps hitting until it falls, and then the way is clear. On the player it attacks the monsters it walks into; on a monster whose **Grid Chase** has **Stop Distance** 0 (with **Layers** `Player`), it attacks the player it walks into. Taking turns, each hit is an action, so a hit and the monster's hit back go turn by turn. Give the things it hits an **Invincible Time** of 0 so each hit lands. Scripts hear its `Hit` event; in a network game the host does the damage.

## Swords and close-up attacks

Select the player and choose **Entity → Characters → Add Attack**: it gets an **Attack** and a `Hitbox` child to its right, a trigger on the `Attack` layer with a **DamageOnTouch** that hurts enemies. Pressing the Attack action (X, L, the right mouse button or the gamepad's X) winds up for **Windup** seconds, brings the hitbox out for **Active Time** seconds (it's hidden the rest of the time), then rests for **Cooldown**; a press meanwhile swings as soon as it can. Each swing hurts each thing once, however long it lingers on it, and a hit freezes the game for **Hit Stop** seconds so it lands hard. The hitbox swings the way its owner faces (laid out facing right, it's mirrored to face left, and turned up and down in a top-down game). **Lunge** steps forward as it strikes, a **Sound** plays, and an **Animation** with an `Attack` clip (`AttackUp` and `AttackDown` too, top-down) plays it. An enemy's attack has no **Action**: it swings at its **Target** (`Player`) once it's within **Range**, turning to face it first, so a knight is a **Patrol**, a **Chase** and an **Attack** with a hitbox that hurts the `Player` layer.

## Turrets and guns

A **Shooter** fires its **Prefab** (`Bullet`, which needs a Body) every **Interval** seconds at **Speed**: at its **Target** while it's within **Range**, or in its **Direction**. **Count** and **Spread** fire a fan of shots (or with one shot, wobble its aim), **Offset** is where shots appear, and a **Sound** plays with each volley. Set its **Action** to `Fire` and it's the player's gun instead: it fires while Fire is held, towards the right stick or the mouse. Shots are turned to face the way they fly. Set its **Beats** to fire on the music's beat instead of every **Interval** (see [Keeping the beat](/docs/audio/#keeping-the-beat)).

## Checkpoints

Tick a player's **Health**'s **Respawn** and losing its hit points doesn't destroy it: it disappears for **Respawn Delay** seconds, then comes back with full health (and a moment of invincibility) at the last **Checkpoint** it touched, or where it started. A checkpoint is a trigger with a **Checkpoint**: touching it makes it the **Active** one, turns its sprite its **Active Tint**, plays its **Sound** and sends its **Message**. Bottomless pits and lava are a **DamageOnTouch** with **Kill** ticked, which kills straight through invincibility. Cameras following the player jump straight to where it comes back.
