## Menus and HUDs

**Entity → UI → Button** makes a clickable button: a background sprite, a label and a **Button** component. Set its **Action** and a menu needs no code at all: **LoadScene** (with **Scene**, e.g. `Game`), **RestartScene** or **Quit**, with a **Fade** (in seconds) to fade the screen out and back in around the switch. A **Shortcut** key (Enter for Play, Esc for Back) clicks it too. **Do** does any action of the entities named in **Targets**, as a Trigger does (an RPG hero's **Attack**, **Cast Spell** or **Use Item**, a door's **Open**, with **With** for an action that takes a value), and is faded while none of them can do it now (a fighter off its turn or without the mana for its spell; a script component says so by implementing `IActionReady`). Add a **ScreenAnchor** to pin anything to an edge or corner of the screen, like a score in the top-left: it stays put whatever the window's shape, while the camera follows the player or shakes. The Scene view outlines buttons' clickable areas in blue. Menus work without a mouse: the arrow keys, WASD, D-pad or stick pick a button (it lights up), and Enter, Space or the A button click it; with a controller, the top button is picked straight away, and B clicks whichever button Esc would. Untick a button's **Navigable** if it sits on a HUD during play, so moving the player doesn't pick it.

## Options menus

**Entity → UI → Slider** and **Toggle** make an options menu with no code. Set a slider's **Setting** to **Volume**, **MusicVolume**, **SoundVolume** or **AutoSpeed** (how quickly dialogue reads on by itself), or a toggle's to **Sound** (on or off), **Fullscreen**, **SkipUnread** (whether skipping goes through lines not read yet), or **Skip** or **Auto** (dialogue skipping or reading on by itself now; see [Skip and Auto](/docs/visual-novels/#skip-and-auto)), and it changes that setting and always shows it. The game remembers the player's choices (whether made by a slider, a toggle or a script) and puts them back next time it runs. For anything else, like a difficulty, set **Save As** to a name instead and read it from scripts with `Game.SaveData.GetFloat("difficulty")`. A slider is dragged with the mouse, turned with the wheel, or moved by its **Step** with left and right once picked; a toggle is a button whose **On Text** and **Off Text** become its label ("Sound: On"). Built games go fullscreen with F11 too.

## Pause menus and panels

**Entity → UI → Panel** makes a panel: a backdrop pinned to the middle of the screen. Select it and create buttons, sliders and choices to put them on it. While the game runs it's hidden until it opens (tick **Open** to have it open from the start), so you can lay it out in the Scene view. Set its **Open On** to `Pause` and it's a pause menu with no code: Esc, P or Start opens it and stops the game, and pressing it again, Esc or the gamepad's B closes it (if the panel has a button with the Esc **Shortcut**, Esc clicks that instead). A button's **Action** opens and closes panels too: **OpenPanel** (with **Panel** naming one, like `Options`) and **ClosePanel** (Resume, Back). **SaveGame**, **LoadGame** and **DeleteSave** (with a **Slot**, or on a **Save Slot** that shows it), **QuickSave** and **QuickLoad** save the game and load it again, the scene, the talk and the flags as they were (see [Saving and loading](/docs/visual-novels/#saving-and-loading)). While a panel is open, the mouse, keyboard and gamepad only reach the controls on it, so the HUD and the menu underneath don't react; its top control (or its **First**) is picked straight away, and closing it gives the keyboard back to what was picked before. Panels stack: an options panel inside the pause menu closes with it. **Pause Game** (on by default) stops game time and runs only the scripts on open panels, and Interact doesn't reach levers, doors or dialogue behind it; untick it for a game over screen that the game goes on behind. **Pauses** names what it stops instead of the whole game: `Arcade` stops only that entity and everything inside it (a minigame on a cabinet, the world behind a shop) while the rest runs on behind the panel (see [Pausing part of the game](/docs/ui/#pausing-part-of-the-game)). **Close On Back** off makes the player choose their way out. **Open Message** opens it when that message is sent, with no code: `Solved` from a puzzle's goals, a `GameOver` from a player's **Death Message**, or anything a pickup, switch or script sends. **Needs Flag** keeps it shut until a flag is set (`FoundMap`; `not FoundMap` for while it isn't): its **Open On** key, **Open Message** and buttons that open it do nothing until then, though its **Open** action and scripts still open it.

## Pausing part of the game

A **Pause Group** on an entity makes it and everything inside it a group that pauses on its own: a match-3 minigame on an arcade cabinet in a platformer, one player's half of a split screen, the enemies while a boss talks. While its **Paused** is ticked, nothing in the group moves, falls, animates, emits, counts down, spawns, shoots, runs its scripts or takes the player's input (a board in it stands still, a player in it doesn't move on the arrow keys), but it's still drawn and still solid to what's outside it, and the rest of the game runs on. Its **Pause**, **Resume** and **Toggle** actions let a trigger, a wire or a script do it with no code ("when `Insert Coin` arrives, Resume the Arcade"), and a switch or a wire into it runs it while on and pauses it while off. **Pause Message** and **Resume Message** go out as it changes, and its output **Paused** can be shown or wired. A panel's or a dialogue's **Pauses** pauses a group the same way while it's open or being said. Scripts call `Game.Pause(entity)` and `Game.Resume(entity)` and read `entity.IsPaused`. What's paused is worked out on every step from the pause groups, the open panels and the dialogue being said, so rewinding puts it back as it was; online, the host pauses groups as it runs everything else (a panel still can't pause anything there).

## Button prompts

Put an action's name in braces in any text and it shows the key or button that does it, on whatever the player is using: `Press {Interact}` reads "Press E" on the keyboard and "Press Y" on a gamepad, and follows the player's own bindings. Braces around anything else are left alone.

## Controls screens

A button whose **Action** is **Rebind** and whose **Input Action** is `Jump` waits for the next key or gamepad button when clicked and makes it jump from then on (a key replaces Jump's keys, a gamepad button its buttons; Esc cancels). It's remembered next time. Label it `Jump: {Jump}` and it always shows the binding (`...` while it waits). **ResetControls** puts every action back. **Entity → UI → Choice** makes a button that picks one of its **Options** (one per row): left and right change it while it's picked, a click or the mouse wheel moves to the next, and its **Format** (`Difficulty: < {0} >`) labels it. With **Save As** (`difficulty`) it's remembered, for scripts to read with `Game.SaveData.GetString("difficulty")`.

## Buttons that can't be used yet

Untick a button's **Interactable** and it's drawn faded, ignores the mouse and its shortcut, and is skipped by the arrow keys. **Needs Saved** keeps it that way until something is saved under that name, for a Continue button that waits for a saved game.

## New game and carrying on

A button whose **Action** is **NewGame** starts the game over: it forgets the progress kept in the save data (flags, saved inventories, what menus saved, best scores) but not the player's own settings and controls (volumes, fullscreen, rebound keys), saved games or the lines they've read, and goes to its **Scene**. Give it a **Needs Saved** and it waits until there's something to forget. A **LoadScene** button's **Saved Scene** names a value in the save data holding a scene to go to instead of its **Scene**: a Play button that carries on where the player left off, which goes to **Scene** until something's saved there (an adventure's walker keeps the room it's in with its **Save Room As**; see [Rooms](/docs/adventures/#rooms)). The Adventure template's menu has both.

## Nine-slice panels

Set a sprite's **Slice** to how much of its image is border (say 0.25 for a quarter at each edge) and its **Slice Size** to how big the border is drawn: its corners then stay crisp however you stretch its **Size**, for menu panels, dialogue boxes and buttons from one small image.

## HUD bars

**Entity → UI → Bar** makes a bar that fills to its **Value** out of **Max**, which way its **Direction** says. Set its **Track** to an entity's name (`Player`, `Boss`) and it shows that entity's health with no code; give it an **Output** too and it shows any of that entity's outputs (see [Outputs](/docs/story/#outputs)), out of what the output says it's out of (a match board's **Score** fills towards its **Target Score**) or the bar's own **Max**: a turn's **Time Left**, a faction's **Amount:Gold**. When it goes down, the part just lost lingers in the **Trail** color for a moment before draining away.

## Pickups and scores

Add a **Pickup** to a coin (with a trigger collider) and it's collected when the player touches it: it sends its **Message** (`Score`) with its **Amount**, plays its **Sound**, spawns its **Effect** (a sparkle prefab, in the coin's color) and disappears. **Layers** says who can collect it (`Player`; empty is anything with a body), and **Spin** turns it while it waits. A **Counter** on a text adds up its **Message** by itself, whatever sends it (a pickup, `Game.Send("Score", 10)`, an enemy's **Death Message**), and shows it with its **Format** (`Score: {0}`, `{1}` is the best; a counter that counts goals, moves or a match board's gems has `{2}` for what it's out of and `{3}` for how many are left). With **Save Best As** (`best`) it keeps the best score between runs, so a counter on the menu with the format `Best: {1}` shows it. **One Per Message** adds 1 for each message whatever number it carries (a beat clock's **Beat** sends the beat's number, so a counter of beats would otherwise add 0, 1, 2...). With **Track** and **Output** it counts any entity's output instead of a message (a board's **Score**, the dungeon's **Floor**, a faction's **Amount:Gold**; see [Outputs](/docs/story/#outputs)), with `{2}` what it's out of. **Reaches** makes it do something at a value: once it's **At Least** its **Reach** (counting up to 10 coins) or **At Most** it (counting down to no moves left), it sends its **Reach Message** each time it gets there (a trigger opens a door on it), and its own output **Reached** is on while it's there, for a wire into a door's **Power**. Starting there (or a rewind putting it back there) isn't reaching it.

The Arcade template shows all of this: a main menu, options (with a difficulty and a rebindable Fire button), a game with a pause menu, and a game over panel with Retry and Menu, fading between them. Its bullets use collision layers to fly out of the ship, and its enemy spawner is a coroutine. On a gamepad it plays twin-stick: move with the left stick, aim with the right, fire with the right trigger. The Platformer and Top-down templates' coins, gems and scores are pickups and counters, with no scripts.
