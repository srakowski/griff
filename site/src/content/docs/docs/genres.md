## Real-time strategy

The **Strategy** template is a whole real-time strategy game made of no-code components: blue against the computer's red, each with a town hall, workers and soldiers, on a map with gold mines, trees, rocks, fog of war and a pause menu. The pieces:
- **Faction**, one per side: its **Team** name (everything with that Team belongs to it; different names are enemies), its **Color**, and the **Resources** it has to spend, kept as text (`Gold 400, Wood 150`) so they show and rewind like anything else. A team with no buildings left is beaten; it sends its **Defeat Message** with the team's name. The Inspector lists a faction's resources, and a unit's or building's **Cost**, as rows of a resource (picked from what the factions have and the resources give) and how much; **Team** fields pick from the factions' teams, and a **Hotkey** from the letters.
- **Unit**, anything that can be selected and sent about: its **Team**, **Speed**, **Radius** and **Sight**, and, for training it, its **Cost** (`50 Gold, 25 Wood`), **Train Time**, **Supply** and **Hotkey**. Units find their way around buildings, resources, rocks and solid tiles on their own, keep apart rather than stack, spread out when sent together, and fight back when something armed shoots at them.
- **Fighter**, for units and towers: **Damage**, **Range** (edge to edge, so a melee soldier's is small), **Cooldown**, and a **Projectile** prefab (an arrow) with its **Projectile Speed**, or none to hit straight away. Fighters attack enemies that come within their unit's sight by themselves, the closest first, armed ones (and towers) a little before the rest.
- **Worker**, which makes a unit gather and build: its **Carry** and **Gather Time**, and the building prefabs it **Builds** (`Farm, Barracks, Tower`). Sent to a resource it fetches load after load to the nearest depot, moves on to more of the same when that runs out, and goes back to work after putting up a building.
- **Resource**, something to gather: its **Kind** (`Gold`, `Wood`, or anything else; a Faction's Resources use the same names) and the **Amount** left. It's gone when it's empty.
- **Building**, which stands still and takes up its **Size**: **Team**, **Sight**, **Cost**, **Build Time**, the **Supply** it provides, **Depot** (workers bring resources here) and **Hotkey**. Placed by a worker, it starts at **Progress** 0, see-through, and only trains, takes resources, provides supply or shoots once it's finished. A **Fighter** makes it a tower.
- **Producer**, on a building, **Trains** units (`Soldier, Archer`, prefabs with a Unit), up to **Queue Limit** at once, paying for each as it's queued (Esc or X cancels the last one, giving the cost back). Its **Train** action (a unit it trains, or the first) and **Cancel** do the same with no code, so a card or a trigger can raise an army, and its **Queued** and **Progress** are outputs. Trained units walk out to the rally point, or start gathering if it was set on a resource. Sprites on entities called `Team Color` inside a unit or building take on its team's color, so one prefab serves every side.
- **Commander**, on the camera, is the player's controls and HUD: a bar of resources and supply along the top, and along the bottom a **minimap**, what's selected (with health, what it's doing and what it's training) and a **command card** of buttons with hotkeys. Its **Map Center** and **Map Size** bound the camera, the minimap and pathfinding. **Victory Panel** and **Defeat Panel** name panels it opens when the game is won or lost. Its map is its game's: units, buildings and resources on it are its game's, its fog covers only it, and its HUD is drawn only in its own camera's view, so a strategy game can sit in one part of a bigger scene, or in a [viewport](/docs/players/#viewports). Each Commander plays a game of its own on its own map (two side by side, or two players' commanders on one map, each with their own selection, fog and HUD); with no Commander at all, the units and buildings make up one game. Untick its **Controllable** (or Focus something else) and its controls ignore the mouse and keys while the game plays on.
- **FogOfWar**, next to the Commander, hides what the player's team can't see: black where it has never been, dimmed where it has been but can't see now, with enemy units there hidden.
- **Opponent**, next to a Faction, lets the computer play it: it keeps its **Workers** gathering, trains more, puts up what it **Builds** (and farms when short of supply), trains its **Army**, one every **Train Every** seconds (0 as fast as it can pay; higher is an easier game), and once it's past **First Attack** and has **Attack With** soldiers idle, sends them to the nearest enemy building, waiting for **Wave Growth** more each time.

## First-person shooters

The **Shooter** template is a first-person shooter made of no-code components: fight out of a dark base past grunts that charge and robots that throw fireballs, with a pistol, a shotgun to find in the armory, medkits, doors that slide open as you come near, a blue key for the locked way out, lamps lighting the corridors, a minimap and a pause menu. The level is an ordinary tilemap painted top-down in the Scene view; in play mode you stand in it. The pieces:
- **First Person View**, on the camera, draws the game through the **Viewer**'s eyes (the entity called `Player` by default); on a camera with a **Viewport** it draws only in that part of the screen, a first-person window in a 2D game (see [Viewports](/docs/players/#viewports)). Solid tilemaps are walls, textured with their tiles (and shaded by side), as tall as **Wall Height**; sprites stand on the floor and always face the viewer (a pickup with **Spin** turns in place), hidden behind any wall that's nearer; particles hang in the air. **Field Of View**, **Eye Height** and **Columns** (strips across the screen: fewer is chunkier) shape the view, **Floor** and **Ceiling** color it, and everything fades into the **Fog** color by **Fog Distance**. In a dark scene (the camera's **Ambient**), lights light the walls, floor and sprites near them. It also draws the weapon in hand, a **Crosshair**, a **Minimap** of the walls, enemies and pickups, and a **Hurt Flash** when the viewer loses health. The HUD (anything with a **Screen Anchor**) goes on top as usual. The viewer looks the way its Rotation Z points: 0 is to the right on the map.
- **First Person Movement** walks (W and S, or up and down) and strafes (A and D) at **Speed**, or **Run Speed** with Shift, and turns with the left and right arrows at **Turn Speed**. With **Mouse Look**, clicking the game hands it the mouse (hidden, and kept inside the game) to turn with, **Mouse Sensitivity** degrees a pixel, and to look up and down a little with **Look Up Down**; Esc, pausing or switching away gives it back. The controller's left stick walks and strafes and the right stick turns. **Step Sound** plays every **Step Length** units walked.
- **Weapon**, on the player, shoots where it looks when **Action** (Fire) is pressed, or held for an **Automatic** one, up to **Fire Rate** times a second: **Pellets** bullets a shot, scattered across **Spread** degrees and reaching **Range**, each taking **Damage** from whatever with Health they hit first (every pellet that hits one thing lands as one blow) and pushing it back by **Knockback**, with an **Impact Effect** where they land. A **Projectile** prefab is launched at **Projectile Speed** instead. Each shot uses up one of the player's **Ammo** item (`Bullets`), so a **Pickup** with that Item refills it and a **Counter** with that Item shows it; with none left it plays its **Empty Sound**. A weapon with **Needs Item** (`Shotgun`) can't be used until a pickup gives that item, and is taken out the moment one does. Several weapons on the player are picked with the number keys (their **Slot**), the mouse wheel or the controller's Y. In hand it's its **Image** (or a simple gun in its **Color**) at **Size**, swapping to **Fire Image** for a moment as it fires, with a **Muzzle Flash**, a **Kick** and a screen **Shake**. It fires while its player's First Person Movement has the input; its **Fire** action fires the one in hand once (ready and with ammo) whether or not it has, so a note hit on the beat, a card or a trigger can fire it, and its **Shots** and **Cooling Down** are outputs.
- **Billboard**, on a sprite, lifts it off the floor in first person (**Lift**: a lamp on the ceiling, a fireball at chest height), **Bob**s it up and down, or **Scale**s it.
- **Block**, on an entity with a Box Collider, draws it as a box of wall instead of a sprite, covered with its sprite's image (or color): doors, gates, pillars. Give it a **Mover** and a **Switch** to slide it open, or let a switch hide it.

Three smaller additions came with the shooters: a **Pickup**'s **Heal** gives back health (a medkit is left alone while health is full), **Damage On Touch**'s **Destroy On Wall** breaks a shot on whatever solid it hits, and a **Shooter**'s **Needs Sight** holds fire while a wall is in the way.

The controls: click a unit or building to select it (double-click picks every unit of its kind on screen), drag a box round units, and hold Shift to add to the selection. Right-click to send what's selected: on the ground to walk there, on an enemy to attack it, on a resource to gather it, on a building going up to help build, and with a building selected, to set its rally point. Hotkeys follow the command card: **A** then a click attack-moves (fighting whatever's met on the way), **M** then a click moves, **S** stops, **H** holds position; with workers, a building's hotkey and a click places it (Shift places more); with a building, a unit's hotkey trains it. Ctrl and a number keeps the selection as a group, the number picks it again, and twice brings the camera to it. The arrow keys, the screen's edges and the minimap move the camera, the mouse wheel zooms, and Esc cancels placing or targeting before it opens the pause menu.

## Block worlds

The **Sandbox** template is a world to dig and build in, Terraria style: rolling hills over caves full of coal, iron and gold, trees, sand, and a floor of bedrock, with a hotbar, crafting, and torches to light the dark underground. All of it is no-code components on a tilemap and the player:
- **World Generator**, on a tilemap, fills it with a world made from its **Seed** each time the game starts (**Generate On Play**; untick it to keep and paint on the map as it is). The same seed always makes the same world, and 0 makes a new one every time. **Width** and **Height** size it; **Surface**, **Hills** and **Hill Width** shape the ground; **Grass Tile**, **Dirt Tile** (**Dirt Depth** deep) and **Stone Tile** fill it; **Caves** hollows out tunnels and caverns, more of them deeper down; **Ores** lists ore tiles and how much of the stone they make up (`3 6%, 4 3%`), each later one deeper; **Sand** and **Trees** (**Trunk Tile**, **Leaves Tile**) dress the surface, and **Bottom Tile** floors it. **Walls** names a second, non-solid tilemap to fill with the underground's back walls, which caves show instead of the sky. **Spawn** stands the player on the ground in the middle.
- **Blocks**, on the same tilemap, makes it something to dig and build with. **Items** says which item puts down which tile (`Dirt 1`), and mining a tile gives its item, unless **Drops** says otherwise (`0 Dirt` for grass, `6 nothing` for leaves). **Mine Time** and **Hardness** (`2 0.8, 3-4 1.2`) are how long blocks take to break; **Unbreakable** tiles never do. **Falling Tiles** drop while there's nothing under them, like sand. **Glowing Tiles** light the dark like a Light (**Glow Radius**, **Glow Color**), and **Underground** is the light the camera fades to below the ground, **Dark Depth** down. **Save As** keeps the world in the game's save data, so what's dug and built is still there next time. **Break Effect**, **Dig Sound**, **Break Sound** and **Place Sound** give it feel.
- **Block Builder**, on the player, digs with the left mouse button held on a block within **Reach** (faster with **Mine Speed**), the block cracking as it goes, and puts down its **Item** with the right button, next to another block and never on top of anyone. In first person it works on the block straight ahead, Minecraft style. What's dug goes into the player's **Inventory**.
- **Hotbar** shows the inventory in **Slots** along the bottom of the screen, each block drawn with its own tile, and picks one with the number keys, the mouse wheel or a click: that's what the builder puts down. Items keep their slot until they run out. **Colors** colors items that aren't blocks. Its **Next**, **Previous** and **Pick** (a slot, from 1) actions pick with no code.
- **Crafting** lists **Recipes** like `Torch 4 = Wood + Coal`, opened and closed with its **Key** (C); click one to make it, lit up when there's enough. `@ Workbench` needs a workbench within **Station Reach**: a placed block of that item, or an entity with that name. The Inspector lists recipes as rows: what it makes and how many, the workbench, and what it takes as items with how many; the blocks' **Items**, **Drops** and **Hardness** are rows with their tiles shown and picked off the tileset.

Tilemaps now only draw the tiles in view, so worlds can be big.

## Contraptions and wiring

The **Contraption** template is a room to build machines in, Garry's Mod and Wiremod style: a lamp on a rope with a button, a lift of thrusters welded under a plank, a door held shut by logic until a crate is dropped on its plate, a clock blinking a lamp and counting on a display, and a windmill turned by its hinge's motor. Its props all rotate, so what's built with them tips, tumbles and swings, and its **Wheel** hinged under a plank makes a cart (wire a button to a wheel's Power to drive it). The pieces:
- **Sandbox Tools**, on the player, are tools to build with while the game runs (with **Within** set, only on what's inside that entity or standing in its area, spawning only there; the tools in use are the first with **Controllable** ticked, so Focus picks between sets), picked along the top of the screen, with the number keys or the mouse wheel. **Grab** carries things to the mouse (**Grab Strength**) and throws them when let go; right-click to freeze one in the air, or free a frozen one. **Spawn** puts down the prop picked from **Props** (prefab names; each gets a name of its own). **Weld** and **Rope** join the two things clicked; **Hinge** hinges the first thing clicked where it was clicked (making it rotate) to the second, or to the world where nothing is clicked; **Wire** joins an output of one to an input of another, asking which when there's a choice; **Remove** takes something away with its welds, hinges, ropes and wires. **Undo Key** (Z) undoes what was made, newest first. What's made is part of the scene, so **Keep Play Changes** brings a contraption back to the editor.
- **Body** has three new settings: **Frozen** holds it still where it is (others still bump into it), **Mass** says how heavy it is, and **Friction** stops it sliding along the ground.
- **Weld** sticks its body **To** another entity, by name: everything welded together is one body, as heavy as all of it (so a balloon welded to a crate lifts it), whose members don't bump into each other. When all its bodies rotate it turns round where its weight balances: a thruster off its middle spins it, it tips over edges and lands on its side, rests and falls asleep, and meets tile floors, slopes, polygons, hex walls and isometric walls like any body that turns; when any of them doesn't, it stays upright. Welded to something without a body (or with a member frozen), it holds still. Hinge wheels to it to make a cart (see [Hinges](/docs/players/#hinges)). The **Rope** tool ties the two clicked together with a **Rope** from where each was clicked, as long as they are apart: it sags, swings and wraps round things, and holds them no further apart (see [Ropes](/docs/players/#ropes)). The Contraption template hangs its lamp on one, ties a balloon to a crate with another, and hangs a third from the ceiling for the player to jump to, climb and swing on.
- **Thruster** pushes its body (and what it's welded to) along its **Direction**, turned with the entity, with **Force** times its **Power** input; a particle emitter on it fires while it pushes.
- **Wire**, on the entity it drives, carries the **Output** of the entity named in **From** into this one's **Input**, drawn from one to the other (brighter while it's on, unless it isn't **Visible**). Every entity has the inputs **Power** (switching it on and off as a Switch does: movers, spawners, doors) and **Show**, and one for each of its actions (**Spawn**, **Fire**, **Damage**...), done once each time the signal comes on; outputs are whatever its components know (see [Outputs](/docs/story/#outputs)) that gives a number or true or false: a button's or switch's **On**, a gate's **Out**, a sensor's **Out** and **Count**, a body's **Speed** and **Height**, health's **Health**, a counter's **Count** and **Reached**, a board's **Score**, a faction's **Amount:Gold**, a script's own, picked from a list of what the entity in **From** has. Several wires into one input add up.
- **Wire Gate** works out **Out** from its **A** and **B** inputs (an input with no wire keeps its value) as its **Kind** says: And, Or, Not, Xor, Nand, Nor, Add, Subtract, Multiply, Divide, Greater, Less, Equal, a Constant **Value**, a Toggle (flips each time A turns on), a Latch (A sets, B resets), a Delay of **Value** seconds, a Clock ticking every **Value** seconds, or a Counter. Chains of gates answer in the same frame, and each is labeled with what it does.
- **Wire Button** is pressed with Interact (E) with the mouse over it, or while touching it (by a character with the input): a **Toggle**, or on only while held. Its **Press** action presses it with no code (one that's only on while held is on for a moment). **Wire Sensor** notices bodies inside its trigger (on its **Layers**). **Wire Lamp** lights from **Off Color** to **On Color** (and turns up a Light on it) with its **On** input, and **Wire Display** shows its **Value** input on its Text, or over itself.

## Visual novels

Visual novels have a page of their own: a **Stage** with backgrounds and the characters on it, at named positions and with expressions, directed by the lines of a dialogue (`[show Ava left happy]`, `[background Park fade]`, `Ava (sad): Oh.`), the Dialogue Editor that picks each line's speaker, expression and directions from lists with a preview of the stage, and save slots with screenshots, a log of the lines said, Skip and Auto, are in [Visual novels](/docs/visual-novels/). A stage's Advance, Skip and Auto actions (Space, Ctrl, A) work only while it has focus, and it names its actions (**Background**, **Show**, **Hide**, **Direct**) and outputs (**Background**, **Speaking**), so a visual novel scene can sit inside any other game. The **Visual Novel** template is a whole short story, Starfall Café, with a title screen, two choices and three endings.

## Rhythm games

Rhythm games have a page of their own: the **Rhythm** template (a whole rhythm game with a song select, results and settings), **Note Lanes** (**Entity → Genres → Rhythm → Create Note Lanes**, with a song and its chart at three difficulties) that scroll a chart of notes from a table to a hit line and judge each press Perfect, Great, Good or Miss against the beat as the player hears it, with the keys, a controller, the mouse or several fingers, holds held to their end, and their **Score**, **Combo**, **Accuracy** and **Grade** as outputs and a message on each hit and miss; each player's audio and visual offsets (how late their speakers and screen are, which beat clocks allow for), the calibration screen they set them on by tapping along to clicks or a flashing light with the keys, a mouse, a finger or a controller, and timing a hit against a beat, are in [Rhythm games](/docs/rhythm/). A **Calibration** listens like a menu (on the top panel), names its actions (**Start**, **Stop**, **Apply**) and outputs (**Result**, **Taps**, **Current**, **Prompt**), and sits in any game's options or pause menu. Note Lanes take focus like any genre's controls, name their actions (**Start**, **Play** at a difficulty, **Stop**) and outputs, and send **Hit**, **Perfect**, **Miss** and **Song Complete**, so a note can fire a weapon or a miss hurt the hero. A **Song Select** picks a song and difficulty to play, with its best score, like any menu.

## Card games

Card games have pages of their own: decks, hands and play zones, drawing, shuffling, discarding and playing cards by dragging them, cards that deal damage, heal, draw and cost mana, and a computer that plays its hand against you, with no code, are in [Card games](/docs/cards/), and the **Cards** template is a whole duel against the computer made from a table of cards. They're built to mix with the rest: a **Hand** takes the player's input only while it has focus, so a card game can sit inside another game, a card's effect can **Do** any action in the scene, and decks, hands and play zones name their actions (**Draw**, **Shuffle**, **Discard**, **Gain Mana**) for triggers and wires, and their outputs (the cards in a deck, in a hand, played) for counters, bars and texts.

## Point-and-click adventures

Walk areas drawn round a room's floor (with holes for the furniture, and depth so the player shrinks into the distance), a walker that goes where the player clicks or taps along the shortest way round, and hotspots that show their name under the pointer and answer a look or a use with a line, an action, a flag or a conversation, with an inventory bar of items to pick up, use on things and put together, all with no code and with the mouse, touch, keys or a controller, and rooms joined by doors; the **Adventure** template is a short one to play and take apart: see [Point-and-click adventures](/docs/adventures/).

## Physics games

Physics puzzlers have a page of their own: a slingshot to pull back and let go (with the mouse, a finger, the keys or a gamepad), shots that fly where its dotted line says with the camera following them, and blocks of wood, glass and stone that crack and break into pieces on hard knocks, levels won with one to three stars once every target is down, and the Physics template, are in [Physics games](/docs/physics-games/). They're built to mix with the rest: a **Slingshot** takes the player's input only while it has focus (and on its side's turn), and names its actions (**Load**, **Launch**) for triggers and wires and its outputs (**Shots Left**, **Out Of Shots**) for counters, bars and texts; a **Breakable** has **Break**, **Damage** and **Repair**, and the outputs **Damage** and **Broken**. The same page has pinball: flippers that snap up on a button and hit the ball hard, bumpers and slingshot kickers that knock it away, a plunger pulled back by holding a button or a stick, drop targets and rollover lanes in sets, a drain that serves the next ball (with a ball save and extra balls) and a tilt, all scoring through Counters; each piece names its actions (**Flip**, **Launch**, **Nudge**, **Tilt**, **Extra Ball**) and outputs (**Up**, **Hits**, **Lit**, **Tilted**, **Balls Left**), so a bumper can hurt a boss and a target can open a door. The **Pinball** template is a whole table, Midnight Midway, with a multiplier, an extra ball, a ball save, a tilt and the best score kept. A **Level Goal**'s targets are anything by name (enemies with a Health in a platformer as well as grubs in a tower), it has **Win** and **Lose**, and its outputs **Targets Left**, **Stars**, **Won** and **Lost**.

## Fighting games

Fighting games have a page of their own, with the **Fighter** template (a whole versus game for two at one computer or against the computer: a character select, rounds against the clock, training): fighters with moves written in numpad notation (`236P`, `623P`, `[4]6K`) over startup, active and recovery frames, blocking high and low with chip damage and blockstun, hitstun and combos, an input buffer and cancels, and the Move List that edits them; hitboxes and hurtboxes on each frame of an animation's clips, drawn over the frames in the Clip Editor, that land hits frame by frame with damage, knockback and hitstun; a sparring pair and a training pair to try them on; a **Versus Match** of rounds, a **Character Select** for players at one computer, and a **Computer Fighter**, are in [Fighting games](/docs/fighting-games/). They're built to mix with the rest: a **Hitboxes** takes no input of its own, hits things with a Health on its **Layers** through their colliders (a hero's sword drawn on the frames of its swing hits a platformer's slimes), has the actions **Stun** and **Recover**, and the outputs **Hit**, **Got Hit**, **Hits**, **Hitstun** and **Stunned** for wires, counters, bars and texts; a **Fighting Character** takes input only while it has focus, does a move by name with its **Perform** action, and has the outputs **Move**, **Phase**, **Free**, **Blocking**, **Blocked**, **Combo** and the rest.
