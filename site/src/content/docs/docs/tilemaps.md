## Painting tilemaps

**Entity → Level → Tilemap**, then set its **Image** to a tileset and its **Columns** and **Rows** to the tileset's layout. While a tilemap is selected, the Scene view paints:
- Pick a tool under the palette, or with a key: the **Brush** (B) paints the cells you drag over, the **Rectangle** (R) fills the box you drag out (shown as you drag; Esc lets go of it), and **Fill** (G) fills the area you click, like a paint bucket: the cell and every cell joined to it side by side holding the same tile, or all empty, within the map's grid. Hovering with Fill shows the area it would fill.
- Hold Shift with any of them to erase instead. Each stroke, rectangle or fill is one undo step.
- Pick a tile from the palette at the top left, with `[` and `]`, or Ctrl-click a painted tile. The mouse wheel scrolls a palette with more tiles than fit.
- The tabs along the palette's top switch between the map's tilesets. Its **+** tab (or **Art → Add Tileset to Tilemap**) adds another from the project's images: a picture painted in Griff keeps its own tiles across and down, and any other is cut into tiles the size of the first tileset's.
- The buttons under the tools switch between the map's layers, and **+ Layer** adds one (see [Layers](/docs/tilemaps/#layers)). The layers not being painted fade back.
- Paint (or drag a rectangle) past the left or top edge and the map grows that way; its layers (and anything else inside it) stay where they are.
- Press Esc (or select something else in the Hierarchy) to stop painting.

## Several tilesets

A map's **Image** is its first tileset, with tiles numbered from 0. **More Tilesets** adds others, one per line with their tiles across and down (`Tiles/props.png 4x4`), and each one's tiles are numbered on from the next hundred after the tiles before it: with a first tileset of 16 tiles, the second's are 100 on and a third's 200 on. A tileset that grows (up to the next hundred) doesn't renumber the ones after it, and every field that lists tile numbers (**Background Tiles**, **Auto Tiles** and the rest) uses these numbers, so `100-103` are the second tileset's first four tiles.

## Layers

A map can have several layers, painted one at a time: a background of walls behind, the level's solid ground, and decoration in front. **+ Layer** under the palette (or **Art → Add Layer to Tilemap**) adds one on top of the others and switches painting to it; the buttons beside it switch back. A layer is an entity inside the map's, holding a tilemap with no **Image** of its own, so it shows in the Hierarchy where it can be renamed, reordered, hidden while you paint the others (its eye), turned off or deleted like anything else; switching to a hidden layer shows it again. It paints with the map's tilesets, tile size and tile settings (**Background Tiles**, **Auto Tiles** and the rest), and the Inspector leaves those out on it, but its tiles, **Tint**, **Order** and collision are its own: a new layer draws over the others and doesn't collide until you turn on its **Solid**, and its **Layer** and **Ignore Layers** set which bodies it stops, so a wall layer can block everyone while a ghost walks through. Giving a layer an **Image** makes it a map of its own again. Agents add a layer with **Art.AddTilemapLayer** (or `entity_create` with a `Tilemap` and the map as its `parent`) and switch with the Scene view's `ShowLayer`.

The map's tiles are also plain text in the Inspector, one row per line and one character per cell (`.` is empty, `0`–`9`, `a`–`z`, `A`–`Z` are tiles 0–61, and tiles from 62 on are accented letters from `À`, then CJK characters from `一`, which scenes save as they are), so you can type a level out or paste one in. With **Solid** on, tiles are walls and floors, except:
- **Background Tiles** never collide: grass, windows, signs. List tile numbers and ranges, e.g. `10-15, 20`.
- **One Way Tiles** are platforms you jump up through and land on.
- **Ladder Tiles** never collide, and players climb them.
- **Slope Up Tiles** (`/`) and **Slope Down Tiles** (`\`) are 45 degree slopes, solid below the line from corner to corner. Players and enemies walk up and down them without leaving the ground, land on them and jump from them; the tall side is a wall, and the flat bottom is a ceiling. Raycasts see them too, so patrolling enemies walk them.

## Autotiles

Set **Auto Tiles** to a run of 16 tiles in the tileset (`16-31`, or just `16`) and paint with any of them: each cell shows the piece that joins up with its neighbors from the same run, in the Scene view and the game, so grass edges and cave walls take care of themselves. The run's tiles go in order of which sides join, counting up as 1, right 2, down 4 and left 8: the first is a lone block, the second joins only above, and the last joins on every side.

The Scene view outlines what collides: one-way runs get a dashed outline with a solid top edge, and slopes a triangle.

## Isometric maps

Set a tilemap's **Shape** to **Isometric** and its cells become diamonds twice as wide as they're tall, the grid's columns running down to the right from the map's position (the top corner of its first cell) and its rows down to the left. **Tile Size** is a diamond's width. Painting follows the diamonds: the brush, rectangle and fill paint the cell under the pointer, the hover outline and the grid's edge are diamonds, and painting past the top edges grows the map up its diagonals. The tiles are drawn back to front, a diagonal at a time, so taller ones overlap those behind them. **Tile Height** says how tall each tile's picture is, in tile widths: 0.5 is a flat diamond (a floor), and 1 a square picture, a block or wall standing up from its diamond as tall again; a picture stands on its cell's bottom corner. A tile with no tileset is drawn as its diamond. A map's layers share its shape and tile height.

Floors and walls go on separate layers. A map draws as one, behind what's on it (its place is its top corner, above everything standing on it), which is right for floors. Turn on **Sort By Y** on a wall layer (and on the camera) and each of its rows is drawn in its own place among the sprites, so a character walks behind a wall and in front of it (sprites sort by the bottom of their pictures, their feet). Sort By Y works on square maps too, a row at a time, for trees and pillars a top-down hero passes behind. Scripts find cells with `CellAt` and `CellCenter` as on any map, `PointAt(x, y)` for a point along the grid (and `GridAt(point)` the other way, in columns and rows), `OutlineOf` for a box of cells' corners and `PictureOf` for where a tile is drawn.

## Walking on isometric maps

A solid isometric map's tiles are walls shaped like their diamonds (turn **Solid** off on the floor, or make floor tiles **Background Tiles**, and keep it on for a layer of walls). A body meets them with the diamond under its collider: as wide as the collider, half as tall, round its middle, so give a character a flat box at its feet. It slides along a wall the way the wall runs on the screen, crates shoved into one stop there, and raycasts (an enemy's sight) and `OverlapPoint`, `OverlapBox` and `OverlapCircle` see the diamonds. The Scene view outlines the solid diamonds, and each body's footprint inside its collider. Tick **Isometric** on a **Top-down Movement** (the Inspector reminds you on an isometric map) and up and down go half as fast on the screen as left and right, as the ground is seen at a slant, so up and right together run along the diamonds' edges; with **Four Way** too, its four ways are the diamonds' edges (up goes up to the right, right down to the right, down down to the left, left up to the left). A **Grid Movement** steps from diamond to diamond the same way, and two ways held together step to the diamond that way on the screen; with **Diagonal**, every way pressed goes that way on the screen (up steps to the diamond straight above). Grid movers face the way they step, flipping their sprites and playing `Up` clips going up the screen and `Down` clips coming down it; chasers, patrols, pushed crates and pathfinding work on the diamonds as on squares.

## Hex maps

Set a tilemap's **Shape** to **Hex** and its cells become hexagons pointing up and down, for board games, strategy and tactics. Each is a tile wide and a tile tall (**Tile Size**), so a tileset of square tiles with a hexagon in each fits: its points at the middle of the top and bottom edges, its sides straight down the left and right edges from a quarter of the way down to three quarters. Rows are three quarters of a tile apart, so each row's points fit between the row above's, and every odd row (the second, the fourth...) is shifted half a tile to the right; the map's position is the top left of its first cell's picture. Painting follows the hexagons: the brush, rectangle and fill paint the hexagon under the pointer, the hover outline, a dragged rectangle and the grid's edge follow the hexagons' sides, and a fill spreads to the six hexagons round each cell. Painting above the top row grows the map up two rows at a time, so the odd rows stay the shifted ones. Tiles are drawn a row at a time, each row over the points of the one above, and **Sort By Y** sorts the rows among the sprites as on a square map; a tile with no tileset is drawn as its hexagon. `Neighbors(x, y)` lists the six cells round a hexagon (the four round a square), and `CellAt`, `CellCenter` and `OutlineOf` work on hexagons. A map's layers share its shape.

## Stepping on hex maps

On a hex map a **Grid Movement** steps from hexagon to hexagon, six ways: left and right along the row, up or down with left or right held to the hexagon up or down on that side, and up or down alone to the hexagon above or below that keeps it heading straight up or down the screen (it zigzags between the two, a row at a time, without turning its sprite). Solid tiles block it as on a square map, and **Diagonal** changes nothing, since every one of the six ways is along a side. `Step(x, y)` takes the same ways (`Step(1, 1)` is the hexagon up to the right), and a **Grid Patrol**'s points count columns and rows, so `0 2` is straight up the screen. Chasers and patrols find their way round walls over the six neighbors and stop next to their target in any of them, crates are pushed straight on whichever of the six ways they're pushed, bump attacks hit what's in the hexagon stepped into, and turn takers take turns a hexagon at a time. **Tactics** units walk and fight over the hexagons too: their reach and range are counted in hexagons (an archer with a **Range** of 3 and a **Min Range** of 2 hits the ring of hexagons two and three away), and the cells they can walk to and hit, and the cursor, are lit as hexagons (as diamonds on an isometric map).

## Walking on hex maps

A solid hex map's tiles are walls shaped like their hexagons (turn **Solid** off on the floor and keep it on for a layer of walls, as on any map). A body meets them with its collider as it is, box or circle: walking into a hexagon's straight side stops it there, and walking into a slanted side (or a point) slides it along that side, so a player running up into a wall of hexagons slips off the points into the notches between them, and one running along a jagged wall follows its zigzag. Crates shoved into a wall stop there and stop what shoves them, and something falling onto hexagons lands on them (**Is Grounded**, with the map as its **Ground**). Raycasts (an enemy's sight, a turret's) and `OverlapPoint`, `OverlapBox` and `OverlapCircle` see the hexagons, so an enemy that needs sight can't see you through a hex wall, and lights with **Shadows** cast them from the hexagons' sides that face open floor. The Scene view outlines the solid hexagons, a row at a time. A body standing still isn't pushed, so a grid mover keeps to the middle of its hexagon whatever its collider's size.

## Animated tiles

Set a tilemap's **Animated Tiles** to runs of tiles side by side in its tileset and how many frames a second, `16-19 @6`, and every cell painted with one of them plays through the run while the game runs: water, lava, torches. Paint neighbors with different frames of the run and they won't move in step.
