## Players with no code

**Entity → Create Platformer Player** makes a player that runs and jumps straight away: its **Platformer Movement** runs at **Speed**, gets up to it and stops at its **Acceleration** and **Deceleration** (less of both in the air, by **Air Control**), and jumps **Jump Height** high with the Jump action. Jumps are forgiving: one pressed a moment before landing (**Jump Buffer**) or a moment after running off a ledge (**Coyote Time**) still happens, and letting go early makes a short hop. Falling is a little heavier than rising (**Fall Multiplier**), up to **Max Fall Speed**. Tick what the game needs: **Air Jumps** (1 is a double jump), **Wall Slide** and **Wall Jump**, and **Dash** (the Dash action, Shift or the gamepad's B: **Dash Speed** for **Dash Time**, once in the air until landing). Down and Jump on a one-way platform drops through it. It flips its sprite to face where it's going, plays sounds and a **Dust Effect** if you give it some, and if it has an **Animation** with clips named `Idle`, `Run`, `Jump`, `Fall`, `Climb`, `Dash` or `WallSlide`, it plays them itself. **Entity → Create Top-down Player** makes one with **Top-down Movement** instead: eight directions (or **Four Way**), an optional dash, and `Idle`/`Run` clips (with `IdleUp`, `RunDown` and so on, facing that way). It ignores gravity. **Entity → Create Grid Player** makes one with **Grid Movement**, for puzzle games, roguelikes and old-school RPGs: each press steps it one cell of the scene's tilemap (or the one its **Map** names), gliding there in **Step Time** seconds, and holding a way keeps it walking (after **Repeat Delay** seconds, or untick **Repeat** to step once a press); a press during a step is taken when it ends. Solid tiles, solid colliders and other grid movers block it, and it turns to face them instead, playing its **Bump Sound** once a press; triggers (coins, doors), one-way and background tiles don't, and layers its collider ignores don't either. It starts on the middle of the cell it's placed in, moves four ways (the way pressed last wins) or **Diagonal**ly too (never cutting a wall's corner), stays on the map unless **Stay On Map** is unticked, and plays `Idle` and `Walk` (or `Run`) clips, with `IdleUp`, `WalkDown` and so on. With no tilemap it steps a world unit at a time, between whole-number positions. Untick **Controllable** for a cutscene. When a player is selected, the Scene view draws how far it jumps each way.

## Ladders, crates, springs and crumbling ledges

**Entity → Create Ladder** makes a ladder (a trigger with a **Ladder**); up and down climb it without gravity, and Jump leaves it. Put a one-way platform at the top to stand on: climbing passes through it both ways, so down from the top climbs back down. **Entity → Create Crate** makes a crate with a **Pushable**: players walk into it to push it at its **Speed** (0.6 of theirs), along the ground in a platformer and any way in a top-down game, and it pushes crates in front of it until a wall stops them. It's on the `Crate` layer, so a pressure plate with `Crate` in its **Layers** can wait for it. In a scene with grid movers it's a grid crate instead (see [Sokoban crates and goals](/docs/puzzles/#sokoban-crates-and-goals)). **Entity → Create Spring** makes a spring: a **Bouncer** sends whatever lands on it flying at its **Strength** in its **Direction** (sideways makes a bumper; on a trigger it's a boost pad), giving a player its air jumps and dash back, and the Scene view draws where it sends things. Add a **Crumble** to a platform and once the player stands on it, it shakes for **Delay** seconds, falls away and comes back **Respawn** seconds later, once nobody's in the way.

## Bodies that rotate

Tick a **Body**'s **Rotates** and it turns: a crate landing on its corner tips over and falls flat, a plank hanging over a ledge tips off it, a ball thrown at the top of a tall plank knocks it over, and its box collider turns with the entity (the Scene view draws it turned). **Spin** is how fast it turns (degrees a second, anticlockwise; set it to start it spinning), **Grip** how well it grips what it touches (0 slides off an edge like ice, 1 hardly slips, so it tips over instead), and **Mass** with its collider's size how hard it is to turn. Everything else meets it as it's turned: rays and overlaps, shadows, a player who walks into a crate shoves it along and one who runs into a plank's end tips it, a weight dropped on one end of a seesaw lowers it, and a player walks up a tilted plank like a ramp (a turned, **Frozen** box is a ramp or a tilted wall that stays put). It lands on tilemaps, slopes and one-way platforms, meets a hex map's hexagons with its turned box, and on an isometric map meets the walls with the diamond under it, as everything does there. Bodies that don't rotate (the default) stay upright whatever hits them, as players and enemies should, and every existing scene and template works as it did. Welded into a contraption, a body moves with it without turning.

## Cameras

Add a **Follow** to the camera and set its **Target** (`Player`) and it follows with no code. **Smoothing** sets how quickly it catches up (0 sticks exactly), **Offset** shifts the view (say `0, 2` to see more above the hero), **Dead Zone** is a box the target can move about in without the camera moving (a tall one keeps a platformer's camera still through jumps), and **Look Ahead** leads the way the target is moving. Set **Stay Inside** to the name of the level's tilemap, a big box collider or a backdrop sprite, and the camera never shows past its edges. Follow works on anything, not just cameras: a pet that trails its owner. For top-down games, tick the camera's **Sort By Y** so whatever is lower on screen draws in front. A sprite sorts by the bottom of its picture, where it stands, so a tall tree and the hero beside it sort by their feet.
