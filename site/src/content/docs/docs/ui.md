## Menus and HUDs

**Entity → UI → Button** makes a clickable button: a background sprite, a label and a **Button** component. Set its **Action** and a menu needs no code at all: **LoadScene** (with **Scene**, e.g. `Game`), **RestartScene** or **Quit**, with a **Fade** (in seconds) to fade the screen out and back in around the switch. A **Shortcut** key (Enter for Play, Esc for Back) clicks it too, or a **Shortcut Action** does: an input action's name, like `OpenMap` added in **Game → Controls** and bound to M, so the key shows in the Controls window and players can change it with a Rebind button. Either one is ignored while the button can't be clicked, while a panel it isn't on is open, and (for Enter, Space or A) while another button is picked. **Do** does any action of the entities named in **Targets**, as a Trigger does (an RPG hero's **Attack**, **Cast Spell** or **Use Item**, a door's **Open**, with **With** for an action that takes a value), and is faded while none of them can do it now (a fighter off its turn or without the mana for its spell; a script component says so by implementing `IActionReady`). In a network game it's done where the game is run, except on what each machine keeps for itself (a menu, a calibration), which it does there and then. Add a **ScreenAnchor** to pin anything to an edge or corner of the screen, like a score in the top-left: it stays put whatever the window's shape, while the camera follows the player or shakes. The Scene view shows it where the game does, on the camera's 16:9 frame, and dragging it there sets its **Offset**. For a camera that zooms, set the camera's **HudSize** to the **Size** the HUD is laid out at: everything pinned then keeps its size and place on the screen as the camera zooms in and out, and its buttons click where they're drawn. Left at 0, the HUD is in world units and zooms with the world. The Scene view outlines buttons' clickable areas in blue. Menus work without a mouse: the arrow keys, WASD, D-pad or stick pick a button (it lights up), and Enter, Space or the A button click it; with a controller, the top button is picked straight away, and B clicks whichever button Esc would. Untick a button's **Navigable** if it sits on a HUD during play, so moving the player doesn't pick it.

## Options menus

**Entity → UI → Slider** and **Toggle** make an options menu with no code. Set a slider's **Setting** to **Volume**, **MusicVolume**, **SoundVolume** or **AutoSpeed** (how quickly dialogue reads on by itself), or a toggle's to **Sound** (on or off), **Fullscreen**, **SkipUnread** (whether skipping goes through lines not read yet), or **Skip** or **Auto** (dialogue skipping or reading on by itself now; see [Skip and Auto](/docs/visual-novels/#skip-and-auto)), and it changes that setting and always shows it. A slider's Setting can also be the player's **AudioOffset** or **VisualOffset** (how late they hear the sound or see the screen, for Bluetooth headphones and TVs; see [Rhythm games](/docs/rhythm/)), set in milliseconds from its **Min** to its **Max** (say -200 to 500, in steps of 5), or their **NoteSpeed** (how fast rhythm notes come, times what the lanes say: 0.5 to 3, in steps of 0.25). A text inside a slider with **Output** `Value` (`{0:0} ms`) shows how it's set. The game remembers the player's choices (whether made by a slider, a toggle or a script) and puts them back next time it runs. For anything else, like a difficulty, set **Save As** to a name instead and read it from scripts with `Game.SaveData.GetFloat("difficulty")`. A slider is dragged with the mouse, turned with the wheel, or moved by its **Step** with left and right once picked; a toggle is a button whose **On Text** and **Off Text** become its label ("Sound: On"). Built games go fullscreen with F11 too.

## Pause menus and panels

**Entity → UI → Panel** makes a panel: a backdrop pinned to the middle of the screen. Select it and create buttons, sliders and choices to put them on it. While the game runs it's hidden until it opens (tick **Open** to have it open from the start), so you can lay it out in the Scene view. Set its **Open On** to `Pause` and it's a pause menu with no code: Esc, P or Start opens it and stops the game, and pressing it again, Esc or the gamepad's B closes it (if the panel has a button with the Esc **Shortcut**, or a **Shortcut Action** with Esc among its keys, Esc clicks that instead). A button's **Action** opens and closes panels too: **OpenPanel** (with **Panel** naming one, like `Options`) and **ClosePanel** (Resume, Back). **SaveGame**, **LoadGame** and **DeleteSave** (with a **Slot**, or on a **Save Slot** that shows it), **QuickSave** and **QuickLoad** save the game and load it again, and **Continue** loads the game saved last (a title screen's Continue), the scene, the talk and the flags as they were (see [Saving and loading](/docs/visual-novels/#saving-and-loading)). While a panel is open, the mouse, keyboard and gamepad only reach the controls on it, so the HUD and the menu underneath don't react; its top control (or its **First**) is picked straight away, and closing it gives the keyboard back to what was picked before. Panels stack: an options panel inside the pause menu closes with it. **Pause Game** (on by default) stops game time and runs only the scripts on open panels, a line typing out in a dialogue box waits under it, and Interact and the arrow keys don't reach levers, doors or dialogue (or its choices) behind it; untick it for a game over screen that the game goes on behind. **Pauses** names what it stops instead of the whole game: `Arcade` stops only that entity and everything inside it (a minigame on a cabinet, the world behind a shop) while the rest runs on behind the panel (see [Pausing part of the game](/docs/ui/#pausing-part-of-the-game)). **Close On Back** off makes the player choose their way out. **Open Message** opens it when that message is sent, with no code: `Solved` from a puzzle's goals, a `GameOver` from a player's **Death Message**, or anything a pickup, switch or script sends. **Needs Flag** keeps it shut until a flag is set (`FoundMap`; `not FoundMap` for while it isn't): its **Open On** key, **Open Message** and buttons that open it do nothing until then, though its **Open** action and scripts still open it.

## Pausing part of the game

A **Pause Group** on an entity makes it and everything inside it a group that pauses on its own: a match-3 minigame on an arcade cabinet in a platformer, one player's half of a split screen, the enemies while a boss talks. While its **Paused** is ticked, nothing in the group moves, falls, animates, emits, counts down, spawns, shoots, runs its scripts or takes the player's input (a board in it stands still, a player in it doesn't move on the arrow keys), but it's still drawn and still solid to what's outside it, and the rest of the game runs on. Its **Pause**, **Resume** and **Toggle** actions let a trigger, a wire or a script do it with no code ("when `Insert Coin` arrives, Resume the Arcade"), and a switch or a wire into it runs it while on and pauses it while off. **Pause Message** and **Resume Message** go out as it changes, and its output **Paused** can be shown or wired. A panel's or a dialogue's **Pauses** pauses a group the same way while it's open or being said. Scripts call `Game.Pause(entity)` and `Game.Resume(entity)` and read `entity.IsPaused`. What's paused is worked out on every step from the pause groups, the open panels and the dialogue being said, so rewinding puts it back as it was; online, the host pauses groups as it runs everything else (a panel still can't pause anything there).

## Button prompts

Put an action's name in braces in any text and it shows the key or button that does it, on whatever the player is using: `Press {Interact}` reads "Press E" on the keyboard and "Press Y" on a gamepad, and follows the player's own bindings. Braces around anything else are left alone.

## Controls screens

A button whose **Action** is **Rebind** and whose **Input Action** is `Jump` waits for the next key or gamepad button when clicked and makes it jump from then on (a key replaces Jump's keys, a gamepad button its buttons; Esc cancels). It's remembered next time. Label it `Jump: {Jump}` and it always shows the binding (`...` while it waits). **ResetControls** puts every action back. **Entity → UI → Choice** makes a button that picks one of its **Options** (one per row): left and right change it while it's picked, a click or the mouse wheel moves to the next, and its **Format** (`Difficulty: < {0} >`) labels it. With **Save As** (`difficulty`) it's remembered, for scripts to read with `Game.SaveData.GetString("difficulty")`.

## Buttons that can't be used yet

Untick a button's **Interactable** and it's drawn faded, ignores the mouse and its shortcut, and is skipped by the arrow keys. **Needs Saved** keeps it that way until something is saved under that name, for a Continue button that waits for a saved game.

## New game and carrying on

A button whose **Action** is **NewGame** starts the game over: it forgets the progress kept in the save data (flags, saved inventories, health and counts, what menus saved, best scores) and the scenes remembered as they were left (see [Scenes that remember](/docs/mashups/#scenes-that-remember)) but not the player's own settings and controls (volumes, fullscreen, rebound keys), saved games or the lines they've read, and goes to its **Scene**. Give it a **Needs Saved** and it waits until there's something to forget. A **LoadScene** button's **Saved Scene** names a value in the save data holding a scene to go to instead of its **Scene**: a Play button that carries on where the player left off, which goes to **Scene** until something's saved there (an adventure's walker keeps the room it's in with its **Save Room As**; see [Rooms](/docs/adventures/#rooms)). A **Continue** button loads the game saved last, in any slot, as it was (see [Saving and loading](/docs/visual-novels/#saving-and-loading)), and is faded while there's none. The Adventure template's menu has New Game and a carrying-on Play; the Visual Novel template's title screen has New Game and Continue.

## Nine-slice panels

Set a sprite's **Slice** to how much of its image is border (say 0.25 for a quarter at each edge) and its **Slice Size** to how big the border is drawn: its corners then stay crisp however you stretch its **Size**, for menu panels, dialogue boxes and buttons from one small image.

## Lists and grids

Add a **Layout** to an entity and it lines up its children, so nothing has to place them: a board of offer cards, a list of quest rows, a shop's shelf. Its **Direction** says how: **Vertical** one under another from the top, **Horizontal** one beside another from the left, or **Grid** in rows of **Columns**, each in a cell the size of the biggest child. They start at the top left corner of its **Size** (the area the Scene view's resize handles show, centered on the entity), inside its **Padding**, with **Spacing** between them, in the Hierarchy's order. Their own positions don't matter: a child spawned into it goes after the last, one destroyed or hidden leaves no gap, and in the Scene view they're put in place as you add, move or undo them.

```csharp
// A contract board: the layout places each card, no position needed.
var card = Game.Spawn("OfferCard", board);
```

A child takes the room of its Button's clickable area, its Sprite's **Size** or its own Layout's (a list of shelves), whichever is biggest, times its scale; one with only text takes what its letters are guessed to. Set **Cell Size** to give every child the same room (say 2 by 3 for cards). Put the background sprite on the layout's own entity: every child showing is placed.

Tick **Clip** and what doesn't fit its **Size** is cut off at its edges, and the mouse wheel over it scrolls it a row at a time (a click or the wheel there isn't the world's, as on a button). Scripts read how far it can go in **Most Scroll** and set **Scroll** themselves; `layout.Arrange()` places the children straight away, for a script that wants to know where one it just spawned went (otherwise they're placed after LateUpdate, before the frame is drawn).

## HUD bars

**Entity → UI → Bar** makes a bar that fills to its **Value** out of **Max**, which way its **Direction** says. Set its **Track** to an entity's name (`Player`, `Boss`) and it shows that entity's health with no code; give it an **Output** too and it shows any of that entity's outputs (see [Outputs](/docs/story/#outputs)), out of what the output says it's out of (a match board's **Score** fills towards its **Target Score**) or the bar's own **Max**: a turn's **Time Left**, a faction's **Amount:Gold**. When it goes down, the part just lost lingers in the **Trail** color for a moment before draining away.

A bar can be drawn with your own pictures. Its **Fill Image** is laid over the whole bar and shown only as far as it's full, cut off there rather than squeezed, so a gauge's texture or tick marks stay crisp and in place as it fills (from the left, the right, the bottom or the top, as its **Direction** says); the trail shows the same picture. Its **Background Image** is drawn whole behind it, and with a **Background Slice** (and **Background Slice Size**) it's a nine-slice like a sprite's (see [Nine-slice panels](#nine-slice-panels)), so a frame's corners keep their shape however long the bar is. **Fill**, **Background** and **Trail** tint the pictures: set them to white to show the pictures as they are. A script's `drawing.Bar(...)` takes the same pictures as a `BarImages`.

**Entity → UI → Stars** makes a row of **Stars**, so many filled: how well a level went. It shows an output (a Level Goal's **Stars**), a number in the save data (**Saved**: the best a level has had, for a level select menu), or its own **Value**, and with a **Delay** the stars pop in one after another with its **Sound**, going on under a pause. See [Winning a level](/docs/physics-games/#winning-a-level).

## Tooltips

Add a **Tooltip** to anything (a sprite in the world, a button, a card on the HUD) and resting the mouse on it for its **Delay** (half a second to begin with, in real time, so it works under a pause menu) shows its **Value** in a box by the cursor, which goes away as the mouse leaves. Only the topmost one under the mouse shows: what's pinned to the screen (or a button the mouse is on) before the world, then the highest draw order; one in the world doesn't show under a button or a system's HUD, and while a panel is open only those on it do. Moving straight from one tooltip to the next shows the next at once. The part hovered is the entity's button, else its collider, else its sprite, or the **Area** you give it. The box is pinned to the screen and drawn over everything: its **Size** (a line's height, as a screen-anchored Text's), **Color**, **Background**, **Font**, **Offset** from the cursor (it flips to the other side near the screen's edge) and **Width** (lines break between words past it). Name a Text in **Into** (a `Hover Info` line on the HUD) and it says the tooltip there instead, then goes back to what it said before. Like a Text, it can show an output: **Track** and **Output** (`Cards`), with `{0}` and `{1}` in its Value (`{0} / {1} cards`), read only while it's the one hovered, so a thousand machines with tooltips cost nothing until one is. A script sets `GetComponent<Tooltip>().Value` to say anything else (a deck's contract, its cards and when it's due), and reads `IsHovered`, `HoveredFor`, `IsShowing` and `Shown`.

## Pickups and scores

Add a **Pickup** to a coin (with a trigger collider) and it's collected when the player touches it: it sends its **Message** (`Score`) with its **Amount**, plays its **Sound**, spawns its **Effect** (a sparkle prefab, in the coin's color) and disappears. **Layers** says who can collect it (`Player`; empty is anything with a body), and **Spin** turns it while it waits. A **Counter** on a text adds up its **Message** by itself, whatever sends it (a pickup, `Game.Send("Score", 10)`, an enemy's **Death Message**), and shows it with its **Format** (`Score: {0}`, `{1}` is the best; a counter that counts goals, moves or a match board's gems has `{2}` for what it's out of and `{3}` for how many are left). With **Save Best As** (`best`) it keeps the best score between runs, so a counter on the menu with the format `Best: {1}` shows it, and with **Save As** (`Coins`) it keeps the count itself, so a counter saved as the same name in the next scene (or a minigame) carries on from it (see [Carrying inventory, health and counts](/docs/mashups/#carrying-inventory-health-and-counts)). **One Per Message** adds 1 for each message whatever number it carries (a beat clock's **Beat** sends the beat's number, so a counter of beats would otherwise add 0, 1, 2...). With **Track** and **Output** it counts any entity's output instead of a message (a board's **Score**, the dungeon's **Floor**, a faction's **Amount:Gold**; see [Outputs](/docs/story/#outputs)), with `{2}` what it's out of. **Reaches** makes it do something at a value: once it's **At Least** its **Reach** (counting up to 10 coins) or **At Most** it (counting down to no moves left), it sends its **Reach Message** each time it gets there (a trigger opens a door on it), and its own output **Reached** is on while it's there, for a wire into a door's **Power**. Starting there (or a rewind putting it back there) isn't reaching it.

A counter can be multiplied by another: with **Multiply By** (`Multiplier`) everything its Message (and its **Add** action) adds counts that other counter's count times over, so a pinball score counts double and triple while a multiplier counter says so. **Max** stops a count going past it (a multiplier up to 5), and its **Set** action puts it at a value (a trigger setting the multiplier back to 1 as the ball drains). With **Save Best As**, its **New Best** output is on once the count beats the best kept as the scene started (a game over panel's text says "New best!" on it: `{0:'New best!';;''}`), and it sends its **New Best Message** the moment it does, with the count (a sound player's sting, a callout), unless nothing was kept yet. A count changing pops it bigger by its **Pop**, springing back, without any garbage.

The Arcade template shows all of this: a main menu, options (with a difficulty, a rebindable Fire button and an Audio Offset button that opens a calibration screen to tap along to), a game with a pause menu, and a game over panel with Retry and Menu, fading between them. Its bullets use collision layers to fly out of the ship, and its enemy spawner is a coroutine. On a gamepad it plays twin-stick: move with the left stick, aim with the right, fire with the right trigger. The Platformer and Top-down templates' coins, gems and scores are pickups and counters, with no scripts.
